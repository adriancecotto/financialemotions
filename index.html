Money Is Relational

People in the same family may have very different relationships with money. One person may seek security while another seeks freedom, experiences, recognition, or emotional comfort. Differences like these can create financial conflict even when both people have good intentions.

Instead of asking, "Who is right about money?" try asking, "What does money mean to each of us?"

"The goal is not to eliminate emotions from financial decisions. The goal is to recognize them before they make the decision for us."

About this assessment: This educational assessment was developed as a reflection tool for a personal and family finance presentation. Its four primary dimensions are informed by research on money scripts, including the concepts of Money Avoidance, Money Worship, Money Status, and Money Vigilance. The assessment itself is an original adaptation and should not be interpreted as a validated psychological instrument.
